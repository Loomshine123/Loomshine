## The 3-Second Damage Checklist

Before you attempt any DIY remedy or give in to despair, assess your garment against these three primary textile conditions:

1. **Shrunk or Warped?** Natural animal fibers like pure wool, cashmere, and merino have microscopic scale-like cuticles. When immersed in turbulent water, these scales interlock and contract.
   * *Verdict:* **Fully Reversible** with professional fiber relaxers and graduated tension blocking.
2. **Limp & Wrinkled?** Structured blazers, sherwanis, and coats contain floating chest horsehair canvas and thermo-fused interlinings that collapse when submerged in standard washing machine drums.
   * *Verdict:* **Restorable** with precision vacuum steam tensioning and multi-zone reshaping.
3. **Fresh Wine, Oil, or Food Stain?** Spills of red wine, butter gravies, or espresso sitting on silk, georgette, or pashmina have not yet chemically bonded to the inner yarn core—as long as no heat has been applied.
   * *Verdict:* **100% Extractable** with organic hydrocarbon bio-solvents.

## 3 Critical Mistakes to Avoid Immediately

<Callout type="danger" title="Rule 1: NEVER Rub with a Wet Dining Napkin">
  Rubbing wet dining tissues or towels across delicate silk or georgette creates friction that shreds fragile protein fibers and pushes the stain pigment deep into the core yarn.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning" title="Rule 2: Keep Clothes Away from the Iron">
  Applying a hot domestic steam iron over a fresh grease or tannin stain will permanently polymerize and bake the stain into the fabric, making chemical extraction nearly impossible.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning" title="Rule 3: Avoid Chlorine Bleach on Hard Water Minerals">
  Gurugram tap water has high TDS (Total Dissolved Solids). Standard supermarket chlorine bleach reacts aggressively with dissolved calcium and magnesium, scorching fabric yellow permanently.
</Callout>
